## Notes per candidate

1. **Rank 1 — "quick sync" (107).** (1) office + joke; (2) payoff lands in
   the last five words, "mild sense of betrayal" reframes accepting a meeting
   as a trap; (3) "quick sync" is a specific, instantly recognised detail;
   (4) the twist — a meeting title as a contract you accidentally sign — is
   not a joke the reader has met before; (5) one sentence, every word earns
   its place.
2. **Rank 2 — performance review (132).** Strong misdirection payoff
   ("called it resilience"), specific (review, HR), but "crying at your desk"
   is a familiar image and it is the longest line without earning the length.
3. **Rank 3 — printer (83).** Shortest and punchiest; "it can read" is a
   fresh personification twist, but "printer jams when urgent" is a familiar
   observation, so the setup earns less than #1's.
4. **Rank 4 — meeting/email loop (117).** Clever recursion with the payoff at
   the end, very specific — but "this meeting could have been an email" is
   the single most common office-joke setup, so freshness is the weakest of
   the top four.
5. **Rank 5 — calendar (96).** Good recognition flash and the "declined it"
   payoff, but "book a meeting with myself" is a known bit.
6. **Rank 6 — "quick question" (94).** Strong time-jump payoff ("It is now
   Tuesday"), specific — but two sentences, and the "quick question" trap is
   familiar.
7. **Rank 7 — reply-all (124).** Funny escalation with HR replying-all, but
   reply-all is the most overdone office topic in the field; the payoff "to
   everyone" is good yet the setup is the stalest.
8. **Rank 8 — circle back (91).** Neat closer ("professionally"), but a
   definition joke of a familiar genre — more remark than joke, thinnest laugh.

project, a deadline, and a mild sense of betrayal." (107 chars)
